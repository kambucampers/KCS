# Kambu Campers — Source Context Charter

> The governing document for all content on kambucampers.com. Every page in the
> topical map is derived from this file. If a proposed topic cannot be justified
> against the Central Search Intent below, it does not get published.

Framework: Koray Tuğberk Gübür's semantic SEO model —
**Source Context → Central Entity → Central Search Intent → Core Section → Outer Section.**

---

## 1. Source Context

> **Kambu Campers is a Kenyan-registered tour operator that owns and operates a
> permanent tented camp near Sekenani Gate and runs its own safari fleet —
> selling comfortable, en-suite tented safaris priced between backpacker
> camping and lodge safaris.**

### Identity

Vertically integrated **mid-market tented-camp operator**. Not an affiliate, not
a marketplace, not a reseller, not a lodge brand, not a backpacker campsite.

This distinction determines the entire map. An affiliate's map starts with
operator reviews and bonus comparisons. A marketplace's starts with inventory
and filters. Kambu's starts with **the tent and the vehicle, because Kambu owns
both.**

### Monetization

- Bed-nights at Kambu Mara Camp (owned, permanent tents)
- Fleet utilisation — 4x4 Land Cruisers, minivans, buses, the "Cheyat" campervan
- Multi-day package margin across owned accommodation + owned transport
- Ancillary upsells: balloon safaris, cultural visits, extensions

Because the bed and the vehicle are owned rather than bought in, Kambu controls
its own price floor. That is the commercial fact the content must express.

### Audience

Travellers who want **real comfort without lodge prices**. Specifically:

- independent international travellers, 25–55, first or second safari
- couples who reject dome-tent camping but cannot justify USD 400+/night lodges
- small friend groups and families needing en-suite and real beds
- Kenyan residents and expats booking short Mara trips
- self-drive travellers hiring vehicles and gear

### Why Kambu deserves the SERP slot

Almost every page ranking for mid-range Masai Mara accommodation is written by
someone who has never operated a camp. Kambu can publish real nightly costs,
real park-fee arithmetic, photographs of the actual tent a guest will sleep in,
the true drive time and current road condition from Nairobi, and honest
comparison across the accommodation ladder — because it occupies the middle rung
of that ladder and profits from the comparison being accurate.

## 2. Central Entity

**The permanent tented camp safari** — entity type: travel product /
accommodation class.
**Dominant contextual domain: the Masai Mara.**

### Why not "Masai Mara" as the central entity

Rejected deliberately. If the Mara is central:

1. Kambu competes on broad informational destination queries it cannot monetize.
2. It collides with **masaimara.ke**, a sibling property in the same network.
3. Tented accommodation — the owned, differentiating asset — demotes to a subtopic.

With the tented camp safari central and the Mara as dominant qualifier, the
accommodation class stays at the centre of the site vector, the Mara
concentrates it, and every page retains a booking path.

### Entity aliases and query surfaces

The same entity is searched under several vocabularies. All are in scope; the
canonical term on-site is **permanent tented camp**.

| Surface | Used by | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent / semi-permanent tented camp | East African trade | Canonical |
| Glamping safari | Western consumer | Primary alias, own pages |
| Mid-range safari camp | Trade + comparison shoppers | Alias |
| En-suite safari tent | Feature-led search | Attribute page |
| Luxury camping | Aspirational consumer | Use sparingly, do not overclaim |

Preserve the distinctions between these and the rungs below/above. Do **not**
rotate them as synonyms inside body copy — semantic consistency comes from
accurate entity relationships, not density.

### Sub-entity requiring its own treatment

**Kambu Mara Camp** is a distinct named entity, not a page section. It gets its
own cluster, its own `Campground` / `LodgingBusiness` schema, its own Google
Business Profile, and its own review corpus.

## 3. Central Search Intent

> **Help a traveller who wants genuine comfort but not lodge prices to
> understand, compare, price, and book a permanent-tented-camp safari —
> primarily in the Masai Mara.**

This is the filter. Every candidate topic is tested against it. A topic with
high search volume that does not advance *understanding, comparing, pricing, or
booking a tented-camp safari* does not get a page.

## 4. The strategic wedge — the accommodation ladder

Kambu's core information-gain asset is that **nobody explains the East African
safari accommodation ladder honestly**, because every operator selling on it has
exactly one rung to defend.

| Rung | Typical USD/night | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Public / special campsite | 20–40 | Bring your own tent, pit latrine, no power |
| 2. Budget tented camp | 40–80 | Dome tents, shared ablutions, basic meals |
| **3. Permanent tented camp** | **90–180** | **Walk-in canvas, en-suite, real beds — Kambu** |
| 4. Mid-range lodge | 180–300 | Brick rooms, pool, larger property |
| 5. Luxury tented camp / lodge | 400–1500+ | Private conservancy, all-inclusive, guided |

Kambu profits from this ladder being explained accurately, because rung 3 is
where the value argument is strongest in both directions: more comfortable than
1–2, a fraction of the price of 4–5. This comparison framework is the spine of
the Core Section.

### What the repositioning unlocks

Moving from budget camping to permanent tented camping makes the following
topics **on-context** that were previously off-context: honeymoon and couples
safaris, family safaris with young children, older travellers, photography
safaris, and small-group private departures. Margin per booking rises without
changing the central entity.

## 5. Boundaries — enforce these

Never publish:

- lodge-safari content that is not framed as a comparison against tented camps
- Zanzibar, Kilimanjaro, gorilla trekking, Uganda, Rwanda, Ethiopia
- Tanzanian southern circuit; Tarangire or Lake Manyara as standalone products
- generic "top 10 things to do in Kenya" listicles with no accommodation or
  operational angle
- destinations Kambu cannot actually operate
- **any itinerary that does not begin in Kenya**
- backpacker / bring-your-own-tent product as a primary offer (it may appear
  only as the lower rung in comparison content)

### The Tanzania rule

Tanzania is admissible **only as a cross-border extension of a Mara safari**,
justified by a real entity relationship: the Mara and the Serengeti are one
ecosystem and the migration crosses it. In scope: northern Serengeti, Ngorongoro
as a pairing, the Isebania and Namanga crossings, migration-timing comparison.
**Hard cap: five pages.** Exceeding it widens the site vector for no commercial
return.

## 6. Cross-domain boundaries

The network appears to include **masaimara.ke**, **lakenakuru.org** and
**amboseli.ke** alongside kambucampers.com. Each domain keeps a distinct source
context.

| Domain | Source type | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| masaimara.ke | Publisher / destination guide | Broad informational destination queries |
| kambucampers.com | Operator | Commercial, operational, first-hand, accommodation |

Rules:

- The operator site does **not** chase broad informational destination queries
  that the guide site owns.
- Where both must cover a subject, the operator version is written from
  operating experience and carries a booking path; the guide version does not.
- No reciprocal site-network links created solely for ranking. Cross-domain
  links only where they materially help a reader.
- Never duplicate factual content — prices, policies, credentials — across
  domains.

## 7. Information gain — the unrepeatable assets

These are the reasons a Kambu page can outrank a better-linked competitor.
Every Core Section page should draw on at least one.

1. **Real rates** from owning the camp and the fleet — including what changes
   seasonally and why.
2. **Real park-fee arithmetic**, updated when Narok County changes it.
3. **Photographs of the actual tents, beds, bathrooms, vehicles and meals** —
   never stock imagery for owned assets.
4. **Operational truth**: current condition of the Sekenani road, true Nairobi–
   Mara drive time, gate queue behaviour, when the Talek river crossing is out.
5. **Named people and vehicles** — guides, the "Cheyat" campervan.
6. **Internal booking data**: which months, durations and group sizes actually
   sell, and what guests ask before booking.

## 9. Positioning discipline

The stated position is *more comfortable than camping, far cheaper than lodges*.
Content must hold that line in both directions:

- Do not drift upmarket in tone while selling rung 3. Overclaiming "luxury"
  breaks the value argument and invites comparison against rung 5.
- Do not retain backpacker-budget framing. It suppresses the price the product
  can command and attracts the wrong enquiries.

Consistency of source context over time is what accumulates historical data.
Repositioning resets it. This charter should not change again without cause.
