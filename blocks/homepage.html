Step one

Plan a Kenya safari around the way you want to travel

Kambu plans four safari styles: private (your own vehicle and guide), camping (campsite or permanent tented camp), shared small-group (fixed departures, lower cost per traveller) and road or fly-in (how you reach the park). The right one depends on how much control, comfort, flexibility and privacy you want — and on what is actually available for your dates.

Private Kenya safaris

A private safari gives your party its own vehicle and guide arrangement, subject to availability and confirmation. That control shows up in the details that matter on a game drive: when you leave camp, how long you stay at a sighting, which loop you drive next, and whether the day is paced for photography or for covering ground.

It suits couples, families, photographers and small groups with specific interests. It also costs more per traveller than shared travel, because one vehicle and one guide are paid for by fewer people — which is why group size changes a private quote more than almost any other variable.

Explore private Kenya safaris, or compare currently available Kenya safari packages.

Kenya camping safaris

Camping is a style with its own equipment, accommodation and service decisions — not simply the cheapest way to travel. The word covers two very different products, and the difference is worth understanding before you compare quotes.

A campsite centres on a pitch with shared or simpler facilities. A permanent tented camp gives you a walk-in canvas tent with a real bed, an en-suite bathroom and a structured meal and service arrangement. That distinction changes comfort, meals, equipment, access and total cost far more than the label "camping" suggests.

Kambu's camping background and its operation of Kambu Mara Camp give these choices a direct operating context — we can describe both rungs because we run one of them. Read the guide to camping in and around the Masai Mara, or explore Kambu Mara Camp.

Shared and small-group safari options

Sharing a vehicle reduces the cost per traveller. It also introduces fixed dates, shared decision-making at sightings, limited route flexibility and group-fit considerations that a private departure does not have.

Kambu confirms a shared safari only when a compatible departure, route, vehicle capacity, accommodation arrangement and traveller expectation line up. Asking for a shared safari is not, by itself, confirmation that a suitable departure exists for your dates — so tell us your dates early if the shared price is what makes the trip work.

Read the private or shared Kenya safari comparison before requesting a quote.

Road and fly-in safaris

Road travel keeps the route continuous, shows you the country between the parks and lets you stop where you like — but a transfer can absorb a large part of a day. Flying returns some of that day and introduces its own constraints: flight schedules, strict baggage limits, airstrip transfers, supplier coordination and higher cost.

Kambu plans the road logistics directly and arranges selected fly-in components through the responsible provider. The carrier controls schedules and baggage rules; Kambu coordinates the route and the ground arrangements confirmed for your trip.

Masai Mara

The Masai Mara is one of Kambu's core destinations. Travellers choose it for game drives across open wildlife-viewing country and for the breadth of species a single circuit can produce.

Migration timing shapes planning, but wildlife movement and river crossings stay unpredictable. Travelling inside a recognised migration window improves your odds; it does not guarantee a crossing, and no operator who tells you otherwise is being straight with you.

You can reach the Mara by road from Nairobi or by scheduled flight to one of the airstrips. That single decision changes how much of day one is left for a game drive and how the rest of the itinerary is built.

Amboseli

Amboseli pairs elephant-focused wildlife viewing with open pans and views towards Mount Kilimanjaro. Whether you see the mountain depends on weather and atmospheric conditions — early morning and late afternoon are usually the better windows.

Its position in southern Kenya makes it a natural component of routes involving Tsavo or the coast. It is not automatically the right addition to a Mara itinerary: the transfer between the two is long, and on a short trip that time comes straight out of your game-drive hours.

Lake Nakuru and the Rift Valley

Lake Nakuru adds a Rift Valley park to a Mara route without a long detour. Lake Naivasha and Hell's Gate open up different possibilities again — boat trips, walking and cycling among wildlife rather than a full day in a vehicle.

These destinations work well inside a broader circuit, but the number of nights and the transfer sequence decide whether they earn their place. Adding a park is only worth it when it adds an experience you would not otherwise have — not when it simply raises the destination count.

Samburu and northern Kenya

Samburu is a distinct dryland environment, not a smaller version of the southern circuit. Its landscape, its northern species associations, its community-conservation context and its distance from Nairobi all call for a more deliberate itinerary.

It suits travellers who have the time to do the northern route properly. It is a poor choice for a visitor trying to fit one more park into a short trip — the drive is long enough that a rushed Samburu leg usually costs more than it returns.

Step three

Choose your Kenya safari by trip length

Three days suits one destination done properly. Five days can carry a realistic two-destination route. Seven days allows a broader circuit or a slower pace. Ten days or more supports northern extensions or a safari-and-coast combination. Duration is not just a night count — every transfer takes hours out of the game-drive day.

3-day safaris

Shape of the trip: one destination, two nights, minimal transfer time.

Three days works for travellers with limited time who would rather concentrate on one core destination than spend the trip driving between parks. A three-day Mara safari gives you two nights in the destination and game drives on either side of the transfer days — subject to the confirmed itinerary, vehicle, accommodation and availability.

See the 3-day Masai Mara safari, or read how long should a Kenya safari be?

5-day safaris

Shape of the trip: two destinations, four nights, one significant transfer.

Five days can carry a two-destination route when the sequence and transfer times are realistic — most often the Masai Mara with Lake Nakuru, or the Masai Mara with Amboseli. The second destination earns its place through what it adds, measured against the hours the transfer consumes.

Compare the Masai Mara and Lake Nakuru and Masai Mara and Amboseli routes before choosing a package.

7-day safaris

Shape of the trip: two or three destinations, or one destination at an unhurried pace.

A week gives you room for a broader circuit or a slower stay, and the flexibility to spend a full morning with one sighting instead of moving on. It absorbs a weather day, a rest day or a long photographic session in a way a compressed itinerary cannot.

Pace it around experiences rather than a checklist of parks. Review the 7-day Kenya safari and our Kenya safari itineraries.

10-day and longer Kenya safaris

Shape of the trip: a custom circuit — northern Kenya, a longer Mara stay, or safari plus coast.

At this length a route conversation is more useful than picking a package by duration. Ten days or more supports a slower circuit, a northern extension into Samburu or Laikipia, a properly unhurried Mara stay, or a safari followed by time on the coast.

What it costs

What does a Kenya safari cost?

There is no single Kenya safari price. A total is built from duration, park or conservancy fees, accommodation, vehicle and guide, group size, season, activities and transport mode — which is why two seven-day safaris can differ by thousands of dollars and both be honestly quoted.

duration + park or conservancy fees + accommodation + vehicle and guide + group size + season + activities + road or fly-in transport

Accommodation is usually the largest single variable, and it moves in steps rather than smoothly. The table below is the ladder your quote is priced against.
