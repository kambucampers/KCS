Sarova Mara Game Camp: tents, location, rates and how it fits a Kambu safari

The Masai Mara National Reserve covers about 1,510 km² of south-west Kenya (Narok County's 2023–32 management plan gives 1,530 km²), from red-oat-grass plains and acacia–croton woodland to the riverine forest of the Mara and Talek rivers. Sarova Mara Game Camp is a permanent tented camp of roughly 72–75 tents, run by the Kenyan group Sarova Hotels, sitting 2.4 km inside Sekenani Gate on the Reserve's eastern side. Below, we explore its tent types, exact location, published rates, what staying inside the Reserve does to your park-fee bill, and when Kambu books it instead of our own camp outside the gate.

The short answer

What is Sarova Mara Game Camp, and where exactly is it?

Identity, location and the distances that decide how your days run.

Sarova Mara Game Camp is a large, fenced, permanent tented camp inside the Masai Mara National Reserve, 2.4 km (about 6 minutes) by road past Sekenani Gate on the Reserve's eastern side. It is run by Sarova Hotels, a Kenyan hotel group, and offers Deluxe Tents, Club Tents and two-bedroom Family Tented Suites on a full-board basis, with a pool, a spa tent and its own safari vehicles.

On Kambu itineraries it is an in-park upgrade: you sleep inside the Reserve rather than at our own gate-edge camp outside Sekenani, which changes your fee bill and your first and last game drives.

Kambu stay class

What "in-park" means for your days and your fee bill

We sort every Mara stay into three classes because each one changes the itinerary differently.

In-park · Sarova Mara

You sleep inside the Reserve. No gate queue at dawn and game drives can start from the camp drive, but reserve fees run for every day you are inside.

Gate-edge · Kambu Mara Camp

You sleep about 9 km before Sekenani Gate, outside both the Reserve and the conservancies, and pay reserve fees only on the days you drive in.

Conservancy camps

Conservancy fees replace reserve fees while you stay there. Walking and night drives are possible; you pay the Reserve only on days you cross into it.

Why the fee rule matters more here than anywhere else in Kenya. Reserve entry is sold as a 12-hour ticket: USD 100 per non-resident adult from January to June and USD 200 from July to December. A camp inside the boundary means you hold a valid ticket for every day you are there, rest days included. Outside the gate, a day spent at the pool or travelling costs nothing. On a three-night peak-season stay for two non-resident adults with two game-drive days, that difference is USD 400, which can be bigger than the gap in room rates.

How the days run

A day at Sarova on a Kambu safari

How we sequence game drives from a camp just inside Sekenani Gate.

Our Kambu Campers driver-guides live at our own camp near Sekenani Gate, not in Nairobi, and start their game drives through the same gate Sarova sits behind. Their advice for guests staying here is practical: use the camp's position for the first and last hour of light, and do not plan a crossing hunt from here as a quick morning outing.

  1. 06:15
    Leave camp at first light

    No gate queue: you are already inside. The plains between Sekenani and Keekorok are minutes away, which is when cats are still moving.

  2. 09:30
    Breakfast in the bush or back in camp

    On a half-day plan you are back for a late breakfast. On a full day, your driver-guide carries a packed breakfast and lunch.

  3. 10:30
    Full-day loop or midday rest

    Our standard Mara loop runs Sekenani → Talek → Mara Bridge → Lookout Hill → the Tanzania border → Sekenani: roughly 90–100 km on an 8-hour day and 140–160 km on a 12-hour day.

  4. 13:00
    Pool, spa or siesta

    Guests who stay in camp use the pool, the Tulia spa tent or the deck. In-park, you have paid the day's fee either way.

  5. 16:00
    Afternoon game drive

    Short hop out for the evening light around the eastern plains. You must be back inside camp before the Reserve's night-time curfew on vehicles.

  6. 19:30
    Dinner and the fire

    Buffet or set dinner in the main restaurant, or a bush or boma dinner if booked. Night drives are not allowed anywhere in the Reserve.

Crossing days need a different plan. The Mara River crossing points (Lookout Hill, Mara Bridge and the Serena side) are on the western edge of the Reserve, a long drive from Sarova. In August and September, crossings often begin late morning and can mean a 4–6 hour wait. From Sarova, that is a full-day drive with a packed lunch, never a quick outing. We don't promise a crossing on any date, because nobody controls when the herds commit to the river.

In camp

Facilities and activities: what is on site and what isn't allowed

What the camp offers, and the Reserve rules that limit what any in-park camp can do.

🏊
Free-form pool and pool bar

The main midday draw for families and for road-safari guests resting after the drive from Nairobi.

🌿
Tulia spa tent

Massage and wellness treatments by trained therapists; book a slot on arrival in peak season.

🍴
Restaurant, bar and bush dining

The main restaurant overlooks the pool, and the camp grows produce in its own organic garden. Bush breakfasts, sundowners and boma dinners cost extra.

👥
Maasai village next door

An adjoining village can be visited for a fee. Ask your driver-guide to agree the price and photography rules before you go in.

🎣
Fish pond, mini golf and bird walks

A stocked pond and landscaped grounds with a small bird sanctuary. Guided walks stay inside the fenced camp.

📶
Wi-Fi and charging

Wi-Fi is advertised; treat speeds as variable. Dual-voltage shaver sockets are in each tent; bring a UK-type adapter for chargers.

🎈
Hot-air balloon flights

Booked through licensed balloon operators at about USD 450–500 per person, with an early pick-up. We arrange it on request.

⚡
Electric fence, 24 hours

The camp is fenced. That means safe movement at night and a calmer stay for young children, but fewer animals wandering through camp than at an unfenced site.

Also on site: archery, billiards, darts, table tennis, a children's playground and kids' meals, evening entertainment including Maasai dancing, room service, laundry (charged) and a 24-hour front desk (Booking.com and Tripadvisor facility lists, 11 Oct 2026).
Not possible from any camp inside the Reserve: walking safaris, night game drives and off-road driving are all prohibited inside the Masai Mara National Reserve. If a bush walk or a night drive matters to you, add one or two nights in a conservancy, or stay outside the Reserve and drive in on game-drive days.

Timing

When is the best time to stay at Sarova Mara?

What changes month to month is the wildlife, the roads and the price.

Peak tariff · book 9–12 months ahead

July to October

  • Wildlife: wildebeest and zebra herds are in the Mara; river crossings are possible from July and peak in August.
  • Cost: USD 200 reserve fee per non-resident adult per day, and the camp's highest rates from mid-June.
  • From Sarova: plan at least one full-day drive west for crossings; in some years the herds also spread onto the eastern plains near camp.
Shoulder, then festive peak

November and December

  • Weather: brief afternoon showers; roads generally fine.
  • Wildlife: most migrating herds have left; resident cats, elephant and plains game remain.
  • Cost: November is good value; 23 December to early January carries festive rates and the peak reserve fee.
Low reserve tariff · dry

January and February

  • Why go: dry, warm days and good cat viewing at the low USD 100 reserve fee.
  • Wildlife: resident herds; cheetah cub sightings are often reported through to May.
  • Best for: a two- or three-night in-park stay where the fee saving softens the in-park cost.
Green season · lowest prices

March to June

  • Weather: long rains from March; April is the wettest month and roads near the rivers get slick.
  • Cost: the lowest lodge rates of the year, plus the low reserve tariff.
  • Kambu advice: book a 4×4 with good tyres and keep afternoons flexible; June starts drying out and fills fast.

Suitability

Who Sarova Mara suits, and who should stay elsewhere

Read the right-hand column too: we would rather route you well than fill a tent.

A strong fit if…

  • You are travelling with children and want a fenced camp, a pool and a two-bedroom Family Tented Suite.
  • You are on a road safari from Nairobi and want to be inside the Reserve within minutes of the tarmac ending.
  • It is your first safari and lodge-style comforts (buffet, spa, Wi-Fi) matter as much as the bush.
  • You are flying in to Keekorok for two or three nights and want short transfers.

Look elsewhere if…

  • You want an intimate, unfenced camp with animals around your tent at night → a small conservancy camp.
  • River crossings are the whole point of an August trip → base yourself on the western side near the Mara River.
  • You want walking or night drives → neither is allowed in the Reserve; add conservancy nights.
  • Budget is the deciding factor → Kambu Mara Camp outside the gate, paying fees only on drive days.

FAQ

Questions guests ask about Sarova Mara Game Camp

Short, direct answers; the sections above carry the detail.

Is Sarova Mara Game Camp inside the Masai Mara National Reserve?

Yes. It sits inside the Reserve boundary on the eastern, Narok County-managed side, 2.4 km by road past Sekenani Gate. That is why reserve fees apply for each day of your stay.

Which gate and airstrip serve Sarova Mara?

Sekenani Gate by road, about 6 minutes from camp. By air, Keekorok airstrip is the nearer strip, 14.6 km or a 20–30 minute transfer; Ol Kiombo is also used but is much further away.

How far is Sarova Mara from Nairobi?

About 232 km by road via Narok and the C12. Plan on 5–6 hours with a stop. Scheduled flights from Wilson Airport take roughly 45–60 minutes to the Mara airstrips.

How much does Sarova Mara cost per night?

Published references put a Deluxe Tent at about USD 180–270 per person per night, full board, outside peak season, and around USD 380 per person from mid-June to September. For 3–5 November 2026, Booking.com showed about USD 424 per Deluxe Tent per night for two adults, full board, taxes included (checked 11 October 2026). Reserve fees are extra. See the rates table.

Are park fees included in the Sarova Mara rate?

No. Masai Mara reserve fees are charged separately: USD 100 per non-resident adult per day from January to June and USD 200 from July to December. Kambu shows them as a separate line on your quote.

Does Sarova Mara have family rooms?

Yes, a small number of Family Tented Suites of about 129 m², with a double bedroom and a twin bedroom, each with its own bathroom, joined by a lounge. Book them early for school holidays.

Is Sarova Mara fenced? Is it safe for children?

The camp has a 24-hour electric fence, which makes it a calm choice for families. The trade-off is fewer animals moving through camp than at an unfenced site.

Can we do walking safaris or night drives from Sarova Mara?

Not in the Reserve. Walking safaris, night drives and off-road driving are prohibited inside the Masai Mara National Reserve; book conservancy nights for those. Guided nature walks at Sarova stay within the camp grounds.

What are Sarova Mara Game Camp's reviews like?

Strong: 4.8/5 on Tripadvisor (2,437 reviews), 4.7/5 on Google (2,096) and 9.2/10 on Booking.com (52), as of 11 October 2026. Guests praise the staff, tents and food; the most common criticism is value for money. See guest ratings.

What is the Olchani tent at Sarova Mara?

It is the Club Tent category, listed on Booking.com as the "Olchani Luxury Camp Tent": about 62 m² with an extra-large double bed, bath and shower, a larger deck and club-lounge access.

Will we see the river crossing from Sarova Mara?

Not from camp. The crossing points are on the Mara River on the far western side of the Reserve. In July to October we plan a full-day drive with a packed lunch, but nobody can promise a crossing on a given day.

Should I stay at Sarova Mara or Kambu Mara Camp?

Choose Sarova for in-park comfort, a pool and no gate queue at dawn. Choose Kambu Mara Camp for better value, driver-guides who live on site, and reserve fees paid only on game-drive days.
