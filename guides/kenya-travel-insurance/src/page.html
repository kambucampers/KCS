01

The rule

What Kenya now requires, what it costs and what the policy actually pays for.

The direct answer

Yes, travel insurance is now mandatory to enter Kenya

Who the rule applies to, where it is enforced and what proof you are left holding. The eTA application itself is owned by a separate guide.

Every non-Kenyan entering Kenya for a stay of under 12 months must hold inbound travel health insurance that meets Gazette Notice No. 11492, and since 6 October 2026 the eTA portal refuses to submit an application until it detects a policy issued under the Kenyan scheme. The premium shown on both government portals is USD 44 per traveller; the amount actually charged in tests on 6 and 7 October 2026 was about USD 52 once a USD 1 access fee and a payment-gateway fee were added. The legal basis is Section 26(6) of the Social Health Insurance Act, 2023 and Regulation 70 of the Social Health Insurance Regulations, 2024, and the High Court dismissed the petition against the scheme on 7 October 2026.

You can buy the cover in two places: on the eCitizen insurance service before you start your eTA, or inside the eTA application at the new insurance screen. Both routes are set out as numbered steps below. Immigration officers at the port of entry may ask for proof of cover as well, so carry your insurance reference number to JKIA.

What the portal looks like

The insurance service lives at insurance.ecitizen.go.ke under the Insurance Regulatory Authority's mark, and it is the same module that opens when the eTA asks you to Get Now. The form is five short stages for each traveller: nationality, passport, selfie or photo, contact details and travel information. Payment follows the last stage.

Nothing on the page names the underwriting insurer, and the terms you tick to accept were not readable in the tests we rely on. That is a gap in the scheme, not a reason to skip it; we say what it means for your own policy further down.

USD 50,000 is what the policy pays out, not what you pay. Several Kenyan outlets converted the minimum benefit into "KSh 6.4 million insurance for tourists", and the figure has travelled. The Gazette sets USD 50,000 as the floor for cumulative benefits. The price of the policy is USD 44 plus fees.

How long does the USD 44 policy last? Two answers are in circulation.

12 months, repeat trips included

Fragomen's 9 October note and the WKA Advocates summary both report one year of validity covering repeat entries. Officials told the travel trade on 20 August the policy would run 12 months.

Three months on the quote

In VisasNews' 7 October purchase on eCitizen, the generated quote showed a three-month coverage period. Nobody has confirmed whether one policy covers several entries.

Tiered by length of stay in the framework

The Ministry of Health's November 2025 administrative framework describes cover for stays up to 30 days, 31 to 60 days, and private medical insurance beyond 60 days.

We publish all three because they come from sources of similar standing and have not been reconciled by the Government. For a single safari of up to three weeks, any of them covers your trip. If you plan two separate visits within a year, check the dates on your reference before relying on one policy.

What it covers

A medical floor, not a travel policy

The line items add up to USD 51,300, not USD 50,000. That is our arithmetic on the published schedule, and it is not an error in the Gazette: the notice sets each line as a floor and the cumulative figure as a separate floor. An insurer must clear all six. It does mean a policy quoted at "USD 50,000 total" is not automatically compliant if one line falls short.

What the mandatory cover does

  • Satisfies the entry condition. It is the only thing the eTA portal recognises automatically.
  • Ensures a Kenyan hospital is paid for emergency treatment up to the limits above.
  • Pays for evacuation to hospital up to USD 25,000, which matters more in the Mara than in Nairobi.

What is not in the published minimum

  • Trip cancellation or curtailment — your deposit and balance are not protected by it.
  • Baggage, cameras, delays or missed connections — none of these appear in the schedule.
  • Evacuation to your home country beyond the USD 25,000 transport limit, or treatment at home afterwards.
  • Readable policy wording — in tests the terms could not be opened before accepting them, and no certificate was issued after payment.

The Regulations also list personal accident benefits among what the cover must provide; the Gazette's minimum schedule does not put a figure on them. Treat the table as the floor you can rely on and nothing more.

02

Buying it

Two routes to the same policy. We recommend the first.

Route A · recommended

Buy it on eCitizen before you start the eTA

Seven steps, one traveller at a time. Doing it first means the eTA recognises the policy on its own and you are never stuck at the eTA payment screen with a failing card.

Go to insurance.ecitizen.go.ke, choose Inbound Travel Health Insurance, complete the five stages for each traveller, pay about USD 52, then save the insurance reference number and a screenshot of the Valid status. When you later submit the eTA, the portal matches the policy to your passport number and shows "You already have a valid travel insurance". Allow a day between the two in case payment confirmation lags, and finish both at least two weeks before you fly, which is the lead time the eTA portal itself recommends.

  1. Open the eCitizen insurance service and choose the travel product

    Visit insurance.ecitizen.go.ke. The service is titled Inbound Travel Health Insurance and the page header shows the Insurance Regulatory Authority alongside eCitizen. You can log in with an existing eCitizen account, but the form itself starts without one. If you are buying for a family or group, each person is an "insurance recipient" and needs the same five stages.

  2. Select your nationality and read the eTA flag

    Type your country or pick it from the list. Once selected, the portal marks whether that passport also needs an eTA. An Italian passport, for example, is tagged eTA Required; a South African one is not, because South Africa is on the eTA-exempt list. The tag tells you which of the two government applications you still have ahead of you. Press Next.

  3. Upload the passport biographic page

    Stage 2 asks for a photograph or scan of the passport page with your photo and machine-readable lines. Drag the file into the green box or click it to choose one. Use the same passport you will travel on and present at JKIA: the policy is later matched to your passport number, and a mismatch is the most likely reason an eTA would fail to find it. Make sure the passport has at least six months' validity and a blank page, since the eTA requires both.

  4. Take or upload a selfie

    Stage 3 is a face photograph. The portal compares it biometrically with the passport image you uploaded in the previous step, so use a plain background, no sunglasses, no hat. If the comparison fails you will be asked to retake it rather than rejected outright. No screenshot of this stage is shown here because the capture contains a face.

  5. Enter contact details and travel information

    Stages 4 and 5 ask for an email address and phone number, then the dates and purpose of travel. Use the email you will use for the eTA and give the arrival date on your flight ticket. Your cover is meant to run for the entire stay, so enter the real departure date, not a guess. If you are booked on a private safari from Nairobi with Kambu Campers, "tourism" is the purpose and your Kambu confirmation is the accommodation reference the eTA will ask for later.

  6. Pay, then save the reference number

    The quote shows the premium of USD 44 per traveller and the USD 1 access fee; the gateway fee appears at the card step and brought the total to about USD 52 in both October tests. Pay by card. When payment confirms, the service issues an individual insurance reference number for each traveller. Write it down, screenshot it and email it to yourself. In tests on 7 October 2026 no policy document or certificate was issued, immediately or by email, so the reference number is your proof.

    What you should see

    A reference number for each traveller, and under Search Status a result that reads Valid when you look the policy up by passport number.

  7. Confirm the status, then apply for the eTA

    Use Search Status on eCitizen with your passport number and confirm it returns Valid. Then go to etakenya.go.ke and apply as normal. At the insurance screen the portal should display "You already have a valid travel insurance" with your reference, and the button to pay for the eTA becomes available. After eTA payment the application summary keeps showing a green Insurance: Valid line. The approved eTA PDF itself does not mention the insurance, so keep both documents.

Card payments were failing for some applicants on 6 October 2026. Readers reported on VisasNews that the eTA insurance step would not take their cards, which blocked the eTA as well. That is traveller sentiment, not an official notice, but it is the strongest argument for Route A: if eCitizen payment fails, you can retry another day without an eTA application sitting half-finished.

Route B

Buy it inside the eTA application

The same policy, bought at the moment the eTA asks for it. Six steps as the portal behaved on 6 October 2026.

Complete the eTA form as usual; immediately before the eTA payment step a screen headed "Mandatory Inbound Travel Health Insurance Requirement" appears, and if no policy is found you press Get Now, confirm the pre-filled details, pay about USD 52 and are returned to the eTA to pay its fee. The result is identical to Route A. The disadvantage is sequencing: a payment failure here stalls both applications at once, and there is no option at this screen to upload a foreign policy.

  1. Start the eTA at etakenya.go.ke

    Apply only through etakenya.go.ke or the Government's "Kenya Travel Authorisation" mobile app; the portal warns that applications made anywhere else are not recognised. Choose Apply as a Group if you are travelling together; each passenger still needs their own eTA and their own insurance.

  2. Fill in the passport, photo, contact and itinerary stages

    The eTA asks for the passport (six months' validity, one blank page), a selfie, contact details, arrival and departure flights and an accommodation confirmation. The documents list on the portal did not yet mention insurance as of early August 2026; the check was added to the flow itself on 6 October.

  3. Reach the insurance screen

    Screen observed on 6 October 2026

    Heading: Mandatory Inbound Travel Health Insurance Requirement, shown under the Ministry of Health, citing Gazette Notice No. 11492. If nothing is linked to your passport the text reads You do not have an existing travel insurance, with two buttons: Search Status and Get Now.

    Press Search Status first if you bought on eCitizen or think you may already be on file. Otherwise press Get Now.

  4. Confirm your details on the IRA purchase page

    Screen observed on 6 October 2026

    Title: Apply for Inbound Travel Health Insurance, with the Insurance Regulatory Authority logo. Your eTA details are carried across. With no policy on file the page shows Insurance REF: None and a premium of USD 44.

    Check the name and passport number match the eTA exactly before paying. There is a box to accept the terms and conditions; in the tests we rely on, the terms could not be opened from that box.

  5. Pay for the insurance

    The USD 1 access fee and the gateway fee are added at the payment stage; the total came to USD 52 per traveller. Note the insurance reference number the moment it appears, for the same reason as in Route A: no certificate follows by email.

  6. Return to the eTA and pay its fee

    The portal now reads "You already have a valid travel insurance" and unlocks eTA payment (USD 30 standard, processed in up to three working days; USD 100 more for immediate processing). Approval or refusal arrives by email. Print the approved eTA PDF or keep it in the mobile app; it is checked at departure and arrival.

Your situation

Your own policy, the day you land at JKIA, and who the rule does not reach.

The unresolved question

Can you use the travel insurance you already have?

The Government has said both yes and no since August. The portal, as built, says no. Here is the sequence, and what we advise our guests to do about it.

As of 11 October 2026, neither government portal offers any way to upload or register a foreign policy, so in practice you need the Kenyan USD 44 cover even if you hold comprehensive insurance at home. The Ministry of Health's written clarification of 7 August 2026 said home-country policies meeting the Gazette minimums would be accepted once a certificate was uploaded to the eTA. On 20 August, immigration and eCitizen officials told the travel trade the opposite: cover must be issued under the IRA-regulated Kenyan scheme. The system that went live on 6 October follows the 20 August model. The High Court's 7 October ruling noted the 7 August clarification but did not decide which version governs.

  1. 7 August 2026Ministry of Health clarification, signed by Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale. Travellers with home-country insurance meeting the prescribed limits need not buy a Kenyan policy; proof is to be uploaded with the eTA; cover can also be bought at the port of entry from a licensed insurer.
  2. 20 August 2026Stakeholder meeting, State Department for Immigration and Citizen Services. The Director General of Immigration Services and the Director General of eCitizen set out a USD 44 policy built into the eTA, with eTA-exempt nationals buying on eCitizen, and stated that ordinary foreign travel insurance would not satisfy the requirement (reported by the Kenya Association of Travel Agents).
  3. 6 to 7 October 2026Portals go live. Two independent test purchases find a Search Status function but no upload option for foreign policies. Policies bought on eCitizen are recognised by the eTA within minutes.

Keep your own comprehensive policy as well if

  • You have paid a deposit or balance for a safari. The Kenyan cover does not include cancellation; our deposit terms are set out in the Kambu cancellation policy.
  • You carry camera equipment, or you connect through a third country. Baggage, delay and missed-connection cover are outside the Gazette minimum.
  • You want evacuation to your home country. The USD 25,000 transport limit is for emergency medical transport; long-haul air-ambulance repatriation is a different benefit on most home policies.
  • You are in the Mara for several days. Evacuation from Sekenani is by road to Narok or by air from an airstrip; a higher medical limit buys options the floor does not.

Do not rely on your own policy alone if

  • You need an eTA. The application will not submit without the Kenyan policy, whatever else you hold.
  • Your policy is annual or card-linked. The portals have no field to enter it, and the Ministry has not said which foreign policies, if any, the Search Status function recognises.
  • You expect to argue it at JKIA. The Regulations allow immigration to require purchase at the point of entry; we cannot promise how an individual officer will treat a foreign certificate, and neither can anyone else yet.
Kambu's position for its own guests. We ask every guest to travel with their own comprehensive travel insurance, and we treat the USD 44 Kenyan cover as a legal entry condition on top of it rather than a replacement. Kambu Campers does not sell insurance, is not an agent for the scheme, and earns nothing from your purchase; the only places to buy it are the two government portals above.

Arrival day in Nairobi

What changes at JKIA, and on the road to the Mara

Nairobi is the entry point for almost every international safari guest. Here is what to carry through immigration and why we want this done before you fly.

Carry your insurance reference number and the screenshot of its Valid status with your approved eTA and passport; immigration at the point of entry verifies the insurance as a condition of entry, and the Regulations allow a traveller without it to be made to buy cover before admission. No source has yet documented systematic insurance checks at JKIA since 6 October 2026, and none has confirmed a working sales desk in the arrivals hall. Plan on the check happening and on the desk not existing.

Approved eTA PDF, the insurance reference number, the Valid screenshot, your Kambu confirmation and a return or onward ticket. The eTA app stores the eTA; nothing stores the insurance for you.

The eTA portal recommends applying at least two weeks before travel and processes standard applications in up to three working days. Buy the insurance the day before you apply for the eTA.

Why we tell guests to finish this at home, not in the queue

The Kambu Campers team runs its safaris out of Nairobi and meets most guests at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport, then drives them the five to six hours via Narok to the camp about 9 km before Sekenani Gate. Our practical advice follows from that routing rather than from the law: the drive is timed to reach the Reserve with afternoon light left, early flights into JKIA often land before 07:00, and a guest who is sent to buy insurance on arrival is relying on a desk, a card terminal and a connection that nobody has yet confirmed exist. Buying on eCitizen a fortnight out costs the same USD 52 and removes the only part of arrival day you cannot control. We do not promise how an individual immigration officer will handle a missing policy, and we would rather you never find out.

Legal basis

Where the rule comes from, and whether it still stands

The instruments, the court case and the government bodies behind each step, with links to the originals.

The requirement stands. On 7 October 2026 the High Court sitting in Marsabit dismissed the constitutional petition that had led to a suspension order on 24 August, holding that Gazette Notice No. 11492 and its explanatory memorandum were validly issued under the Social Health Insurance Act, 2023 and the Social Health Insurance Regulations, 2024, and that immigration officers may verify the insurance as a condition of entry without taking over the regulator's functions. Each side was ordered to bear its own costs.

  1. 2023Social Health Insurance Act, No. 16 of 2023, Section 26(6). A non-Kenyan intending to enter and remain in Kenya for less than twelve months must have travel health insurance as designated by the Cabinet Secretary for Health.
  2. 2024Social Health Insurance Regulations, 2024, Regulation 70. Cover must run for the entire stay, may be bought at the point of entry, and must include emergency medical expenses, evacuation, repatriation of remains, hospital benefits and prescription medicines.
  3. November 2025Ministry of Health, Administrative Framework for Implementation of the Mandatory Inbound Travel Health Insurance Program. Sets the benefit schedule, the designated-provider model and the tiering by length of stay.
  4. 29 to 30 July 2026Gazette Notice No. 11492, signed by the Cabinet Secretary for Health on 29 July and published in a special issue of the Kenya Gazette on 30 July. Prescribes the USD 50,000 minimum and requires insurers licensed under the Insurance Act (Cap. 487).
  5. 7 August 2026Ministry of Health clarification on how the requirement would be verified through the eTA and at points of entry.
  6. 24 August 2026High Court suspension. Justice Francis Rayola Olel suspends the operationalisation and enforcement of the notice pending a petition by Marsabit County residents.
  7. 6 October 2026Enforcement begins on the eTA portal under the Ministry of Interior's Directorate of Immigration Services, with the IRA-branded purchase module.
  8. 7 October 2026Petition dismissed; requirement upheld. Reported by The Eastleigh Voice and VisasNews the same day.

FAQ

Straight answers on Kenya's travel insurance rule

The questions guests have asked us since 6 October, answered from the sources above.

Is travel insurance mandatory for Kenya?

Yes. Non-Kenyans entering for less than 12 months must hold inbound travel health insurance under Section 26(6) of the Social Health Insurance Act, 2023, and since 6 October 2026 the eTA portal enforces it before an application can be submitted. The High Court upheld the requirement on 7 October 2026.

How much does Kenya's travel insurance cost?

USD 44 per traveller for the premium. A USD 1 access fee and a payment-gateway fee are added at checkout, and the total charged in two independent tests in October 2026 was about USD 52 per person. Your own card's foreign-transaction charges are on top.

Do I need travel insurance to get a Kenya eTA?

Yes. Immediately before the eTA payment step the portal checks for a policy linked to your passport number. If none is found it offers "Get Now" to buy one; without it the application cannot be submitted. The eTA fee itself remains USD 30 for standard processing.

Can I use my existing travel insurance instead of buying the Kenyan one?

Not in practice, as of 11 October 2026. The Ministry of Health said on 7 August 2026 that compliant foreign policies would be accepted, but officials reversed that on 20 August, and neither portal offers a way to upload or register a foreign policy. Keep your own comprehensive policy for cancellation, baggage and higher medical limits, and buy the USD 44 cover to satisfy the entry condition.

What does the USD 44 Kenya insurance cover?

At minimum, USD 20,000 for medical expenses, USD 25,000 for emergency medical transportation, USD 5,000 for repatriation of remains, USD 1,000 for mental illness treatment and USD 300 for prescribed medicines, with a cumulative floor of USD 50,000. It does not cover trip cancellation, baggage or delays.

Is USD 50,000 the price of the insurance?

No. USD 50,000 is the minimum cumulative benefit the policy must pay out. The price is USD 44 plus fees. The KSh 6.4 million figure in some reports is a currency conversion of the benefit limit, not a cost to you.

Where do I buy Kenya's inbound travel health insurance?

Either on the eCitizen insurance service at insurance.ecitizen.go.ke, under "Inbound Travel Health Insurance", or inside the eTA application at etakenya.go.ke when the insurance screen appears. We recommend eCitizen first, so that the eTA detects the policy automatically. These are the only two places; no agent or third-party site can sell it.

I already have an approved eTA. Do I still need the insurance?

Yes, the legal requirement applies regardless of when your eTA was issued. You are not being asked to apply for a new eTA. Buy the policy on eCitizen, keep the reference number, and be ready to show it at immigration on arrival, where the Regulations allow officers to require cover before admission.

How long is the Kenya travel insurance valid?

Sources disagree. Fragomen and the August briefing to travel agents say 12 months including repeat trips; the quote generated on eCitizen in one 7 October test showed three months. Either covers a single safari. If you plan a second visit within the year, check the dates on your policy reference before relying on it.

Do children need their own Kenya travel insurance?

Yes, each traveller needs a policy, and the eCitizen form treats every person as a separate "insurance recipient". Officials said in August that children would carry a differentiated rate, but no child rate has been published. Budget USD 52 per child until one is.

Do East African Community citizens need the insurance?

The Act applies to all non-Kenyans, but EAC citizens do not need an eTA, which is where the rule is enforced, and the Ministry has published no procedure for them. One Mara operator reports eTA-exempt nationals are not currently being asked for it. If you are an EAC citizen on a Kambu safari, buying the USD 52 policy on eCitizen is cheaper than testing the gap at a border.

What proof of insurance do I show at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport?

Your insurance reference number and a screenshot of the "Valid" status from eCitizen's Search Status, alongside your approved eTA and passport. No certificate or policy document was issued after purchase in October 2026 tests, and the approved eTA PDF does not mention the insurance.

Was the Kenya insurance rule suspended by the court?

It was, between 24 August and 7 October 2026. The High Court in Marsabit then dismissed the petition and upheld Gazette Notice No. 11492, so the requirement is in force. The portals had already begun enforcing it on 6 October.

Does Tanzania have the same rule for a combined Kenya and Tanzania safari?

Zanzibar has required a USD 44 local policy from the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation since 1 October 2024, and mainland Tanzania introduced its own mandatory inbound insurance in the first days of October 2026 according to VisasNews and Fragomen. A Kenyan policy does not satisfy Tanzania's rule or the reverse. Budget for both if your safari crosses the border.
